What kind of game?

Not a government simulator —
the government itself.

There is no AI ruler in Parlamentum. Every president is a player, every price a negotiation, every law a vote. Three pillars hold it up:

Nations belong to players

Parties are founded, elections are held, parliaments pass laws. The presidency is filled by votes, not by code.

Last night

“Turnout in the Turkish presidential election passed 41%.”

The economy is player-to-player

Every loaf comes from a player's field, every rifle from a player's factory. Prices are set by the market, not a formula.

The exchange

“Iron climbed 12% after the export ban.”

War redraws the map

Fronts are manned by real players; a lost region is truly lost. If your capital falls, the nation fights on its knees for 30 days.

The front

“Bavaria has fallen — Germany's capital is dark.”

How is it played?

The rise of a citizen

The road from street worker to head of state — every step is a real game system, and every system is run by players.

I

Day one

Become a citizen, work

Pick a country, take a job, turn your energy into production. Your wage is paid by a real player's company.

EnergyWorkWage
II

First week

Climb the market

Found your own company, trade on the local and global exchanges, take a position at the currency desk.

CompanyExchangeCurrency
III

First month

Enter parliament

Join a party or found your own; win an election, propose laws, set the taxes and the borders yourself.

Wartime

Go to the front

Arm yourself, strike at the front, take a region or hold one. Victory is written onto the map — so is defeat.
